01 · Funnel foundations

A funnel is a sequence of steps designed to move a person toward one outcome. For this job, the primary conversion is a confirmed consultation booking, not just a form submission.

Learn the funnel vocabulary

  • Lead: a person who expresses interest.
  • Landing page: a focused page for one campaign and one action.
  • CTA: call-to-action such as “Book a consultation”.
  • Conversion: the visitor completes the intended action.
  • Workflow: automation triggered by an event or condition.
  • Pipeline: visual stages used to track lead progress.
The best funnel is not the one with the most steps. It is the shortest clear path that helps a prospective patient take the next appropriate action.

02 · Map the patient journey

Before opening GHL, define what happens from first click through appointment outcome. This prevents disconnected pages and automations.

1. Visitor arrivesAd, Google Business Profile, referral or social post
↓
2. Reads campaign pageService, clinic location, trust signals, FAQs and booking CTA
↓
3. Chooses next stepBooks directly or submits a short callback request
↓
4. CRM record + pipelineContact created, source recorded, opportunity moved to New Lead
↓
5. Confirmation + remindersBooking confirmation, reminder schedule and reschedule path
↓
6. Staff updates outcomeShowed, no-show, rescheduled or consultation completed

Define the offer with the clinic

  • Which service is promoted? (e.g. consultation for a specific dental service)
  • Which location and clinician are available?
  • Is the first consultation free, paid or subject to eligibility?
  • What appointment duration, buffer and working hours apply?
  • Who receives new lead notifications and handles callbacks?
Never invent treatment claims, pricing, guarantees, credentials, reviews or before-and-after outcomes. The clinic must approve all healthcare copy.

Build it in GHL

  1. Go to Automation → Workflows → Create Workflow.
  2. Select a trigger (form submitted or appointment booked/status).
  3. Add actions: create/update contact, add tag, assign user, create opportunity, internal notification, wait, send message.
  4. Use If/Else conditions to separate booked vs. not booked paths.
  5. Add goal/exit conditions or remove contacts when they book, cancel or opt out.
  6. Save, publish only after testing, then monitor execution history.
Do not send sensitive health information in SMS/email. Keep reminders generic (for example, “You have an appointment with [Clinic] at [time]”). Verify consent, quiet hours, opt-out keywords and local laws.

09 · Write the follow-up sequence

Messages should be useful, brief, accurate and approved by the clinic. Avoid pressure tactics and unnecessary repeated messages.

Example message library (adapt and approve)

Immediate inquiry acknowledgement

“Hi {{contact.first_name}}, thanks for contacting [Clinic]. Our team has received your request and will get back to you during [business hours]. If you prefer, you can choose an appointment here: [booking link]. Reply STOP to opt out of SMS, where applicable.”

Appointment confirmation

“Your appointment with [Clinic] is confirmed for [date/time]. Location: [address]. Need to make a change? [reschedule link]. We look forward to seeing you.”

Appointment reminder

“A reminder from [Clinic]: your appointment is scheduled for [date/time]. Please contact us at [phone] if you need help changing your booking.”

Sequence design

  • Immediate: acknowledgement + internal notification
  • During clinic hours: staff callback task or assigned follow-up
  • Before appointment: confirmation and approved reminders
  • If no booking: limited follow-up only with appropriate consent
  • If booked/cancelled/opted out: stop or switch to the correct workflow

10 · Test the entire funnel before launch

Testing is not just checking whether the page looks good. Test the complete path using a dedicated test contact and a controlled test calendar.

Use test names, test phone numbers and test email addresses you control. Avoid sending messages to real patients during testing. Keep a test log with date, scenario, expected result, actual result and fix.

Optimization ideas

  • Low page conversion: improve message match, headline, page speed and CTA clarity.
  • Many leads but few bookings: inspect response speed, calendar availability and booking friction.
  • Many bookings but low attendance: review reminder timing, clarity of location and reschedule ease.
  • Workflow failures: inspect execution logs, missing fields, trigger filters and permissions.
  • Uneven lead quality: compare source, campaign and service interest with clinic-approved definitions.
Sample figures are fictional training data. Never present them as client results or guarantees. Report the baseline, period, sample size and calculation method.
